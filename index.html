Key Takeaways


  1. What is the tenor of an image?
    We know that the tenor is the real subject being described in a comparison, the thing that is actually there.

  2. What is the vehicle of an image?
    The vehicle is the image a writer uses to describe the tenor, carrying meaning and associations across to it.

  3. What is the ground of an image?
    We know that the ground is what the tenor and the vehicle have in common, the shared qualities that make the comparison work.

  4. How do similes and metaphors differ in the way they present the tenor and vehicle?
    A simile compares the tenor and vehicle using "like" or "as", while a metaphor fuses them by saying the tenor is the vehicle.

  5. Why does the choice of vehicle matter?
    We know that the vehicle carries its own connotations across to the tenor, so changing the vehicle changes how the reader feels about the tenor.

  6. What is an extended metaphor?
    An extended metaphor is a single vehicle that is developed across several lines, a paragraph or a whole text, building its own semantic field.

  7. What is a mixed metaphor?
    We know that a mixed metaphor is when two or more vehicles that cannot logically exist together are used for the same tenor, so the images clash.

  8. How can we tell whether a mixed metaphor is accidental or deliberate?
    We know that a deliberate mixed metaphor shows a clear pattern and serves a purpose, such as comedy or satire, while an accidental one is usually a single slip with no purpose


Words that I Learnt from the Story 📚

Interminable
Meaning: Seemingly endless; continuing for an extremely long time with no apparent end.
Example: The committee's interminable debate delayed the final decision for months.

Commodious
Meaning: Spacious and comfortable; providing ample room.
Example: The commodious library accommodated hundreds of students without feeling crowded.

Cellarage
Meaning: A cellar or a group of cellars used for storage, especially beneath a building.
Example: The merchant stored his rare wines in the extensive cellarage beneath the estate.

Portico
Meaning: A covered entrance or porch supported by columns, typically attached to a building.
Example: Guests gathered beneath the grand portico to shelter from the sudden downpour.

Cast up
Meaning: To bring to the surface, calculate, or mention something from the past, often as a reproach.
Example: During the argument, he cast up a mistake she had made years earlier.
